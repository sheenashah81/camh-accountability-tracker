# Finding evidence for commitment statuses

A commitment gets a progress status (Not started, In progress, On track, Delayed, Completed) only when a dated public document supports it. Without one, the record stays Draft with a blank status. Each status change is recorded with the evidence link, its date and the date it was checked.

## What counts as evidence
- A government progress or implementation report, or a ministry statement.
- A parliamentary or budget document showing funds allocated or spent.
- A WHO, UNICEF or other agency country report that describes implementation.
- A reputable news report that quotes an official or an audit.

A plan, law or policy on its own is the commitment, not evidence of progress. A blog post or an AI-generated summary is not evidence.

## Where to look
1. The country's health ministry site: search its "Publications", "Reports" or "News" sections.
2. The WHO country page and the WHO Special Initiative for Mental Health country reports.
3. UNICEF country office reports.
4. National audit or parliament websites for budget and oversight documents.

## What to send back
For each commitment where you find evidence: the link, the document title, the date it was published, one sentence on what it shows, and the page number. Then the status is chosen from the list above by the editor. If nothing is found, record "no public progress evidence found, searched [date]" and leave the record Draft.

## Matching evidence to the commitment
Evidence must relate to what the commitment actually promises, which is the record's title and description. Check each find against these questions:
1. Does it mention the same action (for example "separate facilities for children and adolescents")? Related activity such as a survey or national guidelines is context, not proof.
2. Is it dated, and from the government, an agency or an audit body?
3. Does it say something was done, started or delayed? Announcements of plans do not count.

**Worked example, Kenya** (commitment: separate outpatient and inpatient facilities for children and adolescents, Mental Health Policy 2015-2030). The Ministry of Health news items on a national baseline mental health survey (October 2025) and on the first clinical guidelines for mental disorders (October 2024) are system-level activity. Neither says a children's facility was opened, so neither supports a status for this commitment. They can be kept as background. What would count: a report, budget line or news item about a specific child and adolescent unit, ward or hospital opening or being planned. Search words: "Kenya child and adolescent mental health unit", "Mathari children's ward", "Kenyatta National Hospital adolescent mental health unit", "county child and adolescent mental health facility".

Always send the plain address without tracking tags such as `?utm_source=...`.
